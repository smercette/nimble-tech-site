Privacy notice: bespoke tools

Last updated: 17 November 2026

This notice explains how Nimble Tech Ltd handles personal data in our bespoke tools, which run at app.nimbletech.uk. It is written for the people who use those tools and for anyone whose details appear in the documents they upload.

This notice does not cover our marketing website at nimbletech.uk, which has its own privacy policy, or BundleRight, which is a separate product with its own notice.

Who we are

Nimble Tech Ltd builds and runs bespoke software tools for professional organisations, such as law firms.

  • Company number 16203504, registered in England and Wales
  • Registered office: 255 Poulton Road, Wallasey, Wirral, CH44 4BT
  • Privacy and support contact: support@nimbletech.uk
  • ICO registration number: ZC304135

Your organisation's agreement comes first

Each bespoke tool is built for, and used by, an organisation that has an agreement with us. That agreement sets the terms of use, what we may do with the organisation's data, how long it is kept, and which service providers we may use. If you use a tool through your organisation, your use is governed by your organisation's agreement with Nimble Tech. If anything in this notice differs from that agreement, the agreement wins.

Our two roles

We act in two different roles, depending on the data.

For everything inside the tools, we are a processor for your organisation. This covers:

  • the documents your organisation uploads, and the personal data inside them
  • everything the tool produces from them: extracted details, reports, exports, comments and matter labels
  • the user accounts your organisation sets up, including your name, work email address, role, and your sign-in and security records

Your organisation is the controller of this data. It decides who has an account, what is uploaded and why. We use this data only on its instructions, to provide the tool and keep it secure.

For our own business records, we are the controller. This covers:

  • support requests you send us, by email or through the "Get help" form in the tool, and our replies
  • the business contact details of the people we deal with at your organisation about its agreement, for example for contracts, invoices and service notices
  • records the law requires us to keep, for example for tax

Enquiries sent through our marketing website are covered by its privacy policy.

What the tools hold about users

We hold the following on your organisation's behalf:

  • Account details: your name, work email address, your organisation and your role in the tool. We store a scrambled (hashed) form of your password, never the password itself.
  • Sign-in and security records: when you sign in and out, failed sign-in attempts, the network address a request came from, and records of actions taken in the tool (for example, who started a review and when).

These are used to let you sign in, to keep the service and your organisation's data secure, and to keep an accurate record of what was done and by whom.

We do not put the content of your organisation's documents into our operational or security logs.

Support requests and our own records

We use support requests to answer them and to fix the problems they report. We use business contact details to run our agreement with your organisation.

Our lawful basis is legitimate interests: answering the people who ask us for help, and running our business relationship with the organisations we work with. The data is limited and work-related, and you can object (see "Your rights"). Where the law requires us to keep a record, for example for tax or to answer a regulator, we rely on legal obligation.

We do not use any of this data for marketing, we do not sell it, and we do not use it to profile you.

People named in uploaded documents

If your details appear in a document that an organisation has uploaded (for example, as a party to a contract), that organisation is responsible for your data, not us. Please contact the organisation directly. If you contact us, we will tell you who to contact and, where we can identify the organisation, pass your request on and help it respond.

AI processing

The tools use AI models to read documents and pull out the relevant facts.

  • All AI processing runs through Amazon Bedrock, Amazon Web Services' AI service, in AWS's London region in the United Kingdom.
  • Documents are not sent to the AI model provider directly, and no document content is used to train or improve AI models. AWS does not keep the prompts we send or the responses it returns.
  • The tools help people review documents. They do not make decisions about individuals, and there is no automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effects.

Where your data is held

The tools are hosted on Amazon Web Services in London, United Kingdom. That covers the application, the database, document storage, logs and AI processing. Requests sent through the "Get help" form are stored there too.

Support requests sent by email are held by our email provider, which stores mail in Switzerland. The UK recognises Switzerland as giving personal data an adequate level of protection, so no additional safeguards are needed for that transfer.

Who we share it with

We use a small number of service providers to run the tools:

  • Amazon Web Services, in the UK, for hosting, storage, AI (through Amazon Bedrock) and security tooling
  • our email provider, for email, including the support address

Each works under written data protection terms. The detailed list of service providers for each bespoke tool is set out in, and changed under, the organisation's agreement with us, so we do not publish a single public list for bespoke clients.

We do not share your data with anyone else, except where the law requires it.

How we protect it

  • Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Each organisation can only see its own data. We test this automatically.
  • Where your organisation has set it up, matter labels are encrypted in your browser, so our servers cannot read them.
  • The service has no direct route to the public internet for its AI or storage traffic, which stays inside AWS's private network.
  • Two-step sign-in is available, and will be required for administrators by 31 January 2027.

Cookies

The tools set one cookie, nimble_session, which keeps you signed in. It is strictly necessary, so we do not ask for consent. It expires after 12 hours.

The tools also use your browser's own storage, which stays on your device: to hold your organisation's matter-label key while your tab is open (where matter-label encryption is set up), and to remember whether you asked for a notification when a review finishes.

There are no analytics, advertising or tracking cookies, and no third-party scripts.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of your personal data
  • ask for it to be corrected
  • ask for it to be deleted
  • ask for its use to be restricted or stopped, or object to how it is used
  • ask for it in a portable format

Where to send a request

  • Your account, your sign-in records, or anything inside the tool: ask your organisation first. It controls this data and decides how to answer. We help it do so. If you write to us instead, we will pass your request to your organisation and help it respond.
  • Support requests, or other records we hold as controller: email support@nimbletech.uk. We reply within one month.
  • Your details in an uploaded document: contact the organisation that uploaded it.

If something goes wrong with data we control, we will tell the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) where the law requires, within 72 hours, and tell you directly if there is a high risk to you. If it affects data we hold for an organisation, including its user accounts, we tell that organisation without undue delay so it can decide what to do.

Complaints

Please tell us first, at support@nimbletech.uk, and we will try to put it right. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113.

Changes to this notice

We will update this notice when how we handle data changes. The date at the top shows when it last changed. We tell client organisations directly about significant changes.
